Your Assistant

A local AI that turns messy thoughts into concrete actions: appointments, to-dos, notes, habits – proposed as cards, applied only when you say yes.

● 100 % on your device

The assistant runs entirely on your machine. Your thoughts, your notes, your calendar – none of it ever leaves your device. The only network contact is the one-time model download from huggingface.co, and only when you click “Install”. After that, everything works offline. Forever.

How it works: dump your thoughts, get cards back

You don't write commands. You write the way your head sounds. Say you type this into the assistant widget:

“dentist tomorrow 9am, buy milk, call Lisa back, want to read daily again”

The assistant reads it, sorts it, and answers with proposal cards:

📅 Appointment Dentist – tomorrow, 9:00, with a reminder before it starts. Yes · Edit · No
✓ To-do Buy milk Yes · Edit · No
✓ To-do Call Lisa back Yes · Edit · No
🔥 Habit Read daily – added to your daily routine. Yes · Edit · No

Nothing happens without your confirmation. Every card waits for Yes, Edit (fix the details first) or No. The assistant can only use the same commands you see in the command palette – it cannot invent actions, touch files behind your back, or delete anything silently.

Trust it after a while? You can switch confirmation off per assistant in its settings – cards then apply immediately. Turning it back on is one click away, and it's on by default.

Setup: three steps, no expertise needed

  1. Open Settings → Assistant. That's the whole entry point – the assistant is a built-in tool like any other, and its privacy light is green.
  2. Let Cardo check your machine. The system check looks at your RAM and CPU cores (and recognizes Apple Silicon, which runs local models especially well). Every available model then gets an honest rating: great, okay, slow or too big for this machine – plus one clear recommendation. No guessing, no “it depends” – Cardo tells you what will actually feel good on your hardware.
  3. One click to install. Pick a model (the recommended one is a safe choice) and press install. This is the one moment Cardo contacts the internet: the model file is downloaded from huggingface.co, with a visible progress bar. Once it's done, you're offline-capable for good.

Right after that, the assistant asks a few personality questions: what it should be called, what it should call you, how it should talk (short & direct, friendly, or detailed) and which language it should answer in. Two minutes, and it feels like yours.

Choosing a model: an honest guide

Bigger models understand more nuance but need more RAM and answer more slowly. Smaller models are quick and light but occasionally miss subtleties. There is no “best” model – only the best one for your machine and your patience. Rule of thumb by machine

  • 8 GB laptop: Qwen3 0.6B–4B, Llama 3.2 1B/3B, Phi-4-mini or Gemma 3 4B. Qwen3 4B is the usual recommendation; if it feels sluggish, drop to 1.7B – a fast small model beats a slow big one every day.
  • 16 GB: Qwen3 8B, Llama 3.1 8B, Mistral 7B, Granite 8B or DeepSeek-R1 7B run comfortably. Gemma 3 12B and the 14B models fit, but they're the upper edge – expect them to take their time.
  • 32 GB and up: everything above, plus Qwen3 14B/30B-A3B, Mistral Small 24B and Gemma 3 27B. Here quality is the only question – pick by strengths, not by fit.

Personality, working style, memory: three plain files

Your assistant is not a black box. Everything that shapes it lives in three readable Markdown files on your disk – you can see (and edit) all of them under Settings → Assistant → Advanced:

  • personality.md – who it is. Its name, what it calls you, its tone (short & direct / friendly / detailed) and its language. Generated from your answers to the personality questions; edit it any time.
  • instructions.md – how it works. The working rules: turn braindumps into concrete proposals, resolve “tomorrow” into a real date, never invent commands, when unsure propose anyway and let you fix it via Edit.
  • memory.md – what it knows about you. Durable facts, one line each: preferences, recurring situations and how you like them solved, background that keeps coming up.

The memory follows two simple rules. First: the assistant checks its memory before asking you anything – if it already knows your dentist is Dr. Weber, it fills that in instead of asking again. Second: you always see what it remembers. Whenever it saves something new, a “Remembered:” line appears right in the conversation – and every entry has a ✕ “forget this” next to it. One click, and it's gone from the file. No hidden profile, ever.

Multiple assistants: one for each part of your life

You don't have to squeeze everything into one assistant. Create as many as you like, each with its own name, personality and job. A worked example:

  • Hanna – your work assistant. Tools: tasks and calendar only. Style: short & direct. You dump your Monday chaos on her, she answers in bullet-length cards and never chats.
  • Felix – your writing companion. Tools: notes only. Style: warm and wordy. He helps you draft, rephrase and untangle texts, and never touches your calendar.

They share one memory by default. That's deliberate: when you tell Hanna you're on vacation in August and later ask Felix to draft an out-of-office note, Felix already knows. Nothing gets lost just because you switched assistants. If you want a hard wall instead – say, a strict split between work and private life, where the work assistant must know nothing personal – you can give an assistant its own separate memory file in its settings. Shared is the default; separate is one switch.

Tool scopes keep each assistant honest: Hanna literally cannot create a note, Felix cannot move an appointment – each one only sees the commands of the tools you gave it. Templates (work assistant, writing companion, planning helper …) give you a sensible starting point in one click, and you can export and import profiles as files – to move an assistant to your other machine, or to share a well-tuned one with a friend. Profiles contain the setup, not your memory, unless you explicitly include it.

Teams: several assistants, one conversation

Once you have a few assistants, you can group them into a team and talk to the team instead of picking who to ask. Behind the scenes a silent leader – always the fastest installed model, because its only job is routing – reads each question and hands it to the best-suited member. Silent means it never answers you itself; you always see who answered, right on the reply.

Example – Group 1: Hanna, Felix, Simon (Simon being a research-minded assistant on a larger model):

  • “Move my Thursday 1-on-1 to Friday and remind me to prep” → the leader routes it to Hanna (calendar + tasks are her competence). Her cards appear, labeled Hanna.
  • “Help me rewrite the intro of my blog post so it sounds less stiff” → routed to Felix. Warm, wordy, labeled Felix.

When is a team worth it? If you have genuinely different jobs (planning vs. writing vs. thinking) that profit from different personalities or different model sizes, a team saves you the constant “who do I ask?”. If one assistant covers your day fine, stay with one – simpler is better here.

The honest part about RAM: your machine can only hold so many models in memory at once. When the leader routes to a member whose model isn't currently loaded, Cardo swaps models – which means a short loading moment before that answer. Nothing is wrong; that's the price of running everything locally, and Cardo shows a loading state so you know what's happening.

Delegation: assistants that know their limits

Even outside teams, assistants know about each other. Ask Hanna to polish a paragraph and she won't bluff – you get a card: “Felix is better at this – hand it over?” One click and Felix takes the request, with the same context; decline and Hanna does her best herself. Nothing is handed over without your confirmation, same as everything else.

What drives this are competences: every assistant's profile lists what it's good at – derived from its tool scope, its model's strengths and its template. And competences are learned, too: when you accept a hand-over, that kind of request counts toward the receiving assistant's competences; when you decline, the system takes the hint. Over time, delegation suggestions match how you actually divide the work – you can view and edit each assistant's competence list in its settings at any time.

FAQ

How much RAM do I really need?

The assistant starts at about 2–3 GB of free RAM for the smallest model (Qwen3 0.6B). A comfortable everyday setup is 8 GB total with a ~4B model; 16 GB opens up the 7–8B class; 12B+ models want 16–32 GB. The system check in Settings → Assistant rates every model against your actual machine before you download anything.

How fast will it feel?

Local models answer in a visible stream, not instantly. Small models (≤2B) feel immediate; mid-size models (4–8B) take a few seconds for a typical braindump; large models take noticeably longer but understand more. Apple Silicon Macs are markedly faster than comparable x86 machines for this workload. If waiting annoys you, choose a smaller model – responsiveness matters more than the last bit of cleverness.

Which models are good at German (or other languages)?

Qwen3, Gemma 3 and Mistral are the strongest German speakers per size class, and handle many other languages well. Llama is solid but English-leaning, especially in the small 3.2 models. Phi-4 and Granite are clearly English-first – fine for understanding German input, less polished when writing it. DeepSeek-R1 answers fine in German, but its visible “thinking” text often stays English.

What about licenses?

Qwen3, Mistral and Granite are Apache-2.0; Phi-4 and DeepSeek-R1 are MIT – use freely. Llama models use Meta's community license, which requires a “Built with Llama” notice (Cardo displays it for you). Gemma models are under Google's Gemma Terms of Use; Cardo shows you those terms and asks for your consent before the download begins.

Why does it load for a few seconds when I switch assistants?

If the assistant you switch to uses a different model than the one currently in memory, Cardo has to unload one and load the other from disk. That's the short loading moment – it's normal, it's local, and it's the trade-off for being able to run several models on one machine. Assistants sharing the same model switch instantly.

Is anything sent to a server?

No. Your prompts, files, memory and answers never leave your device – the model runs inside Cardo, not in a cloud. The single exception is the model download itself: when you click install, the model file is fetched from huggingface.co. That request transfers the file to you; it does not carry your data anywhere.

Can I use it offline?

Yes – completely, once a model is downloaded. Plane, dead zone, deliberately offline machine: the assistant keeps working exactly the same. It never needs to phone home, because there is no home to phone.
